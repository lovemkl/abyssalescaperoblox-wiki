## The Roster's Design Signature: Phases

Before the individual killers, the system they share: phase escalation. Abyssal Escape's killers grow through the round — stronger, faster, more dangerous as time advances — matching the mist's pressure curve with their own. The design consequence: killer rounds have an arc. The early phase is setup — patrolling, mapping, applying pressure without overcommitting; the mid phases are the working game — intercepting dispels, punishing isolation; the late phases are the kill window — the escalation's full power arriving as the mist closes and survivors' options narrow. A killer who understands their own arc plays the round's tempo; a killer who doesn't wastes their strongest moments in its weakest context. The phase system rewards patience — the rarest killer virtue — and it is the single biggest differentiator between the roster's design and the genre's conventions.

## The Maniac

The launch face of the roster — the killer the announcement materials led with and the one every launch-week survivor has queued against most. The Maniac's identity, as the community has profiled it from launch gameplay: the classic slasher archetype translated into the game's phase system — a fundamentally straightforward hunter whose escalation curve rewards fundamentals over gimmicks. His phases escalate his chase and pressure tools as the round progresses, making him the roster's skill-check killer: no exotic mechanics to lean on, just patrol logic, chase craft and phase timing done well. For new killers, The Maniac is the learning pick — his kit teaches the game's universal killer skills, and his ceiling demonstrates that fundamentals scale. For survivors, he's the matchup that teaches mist discipline: The Maniac's rounds are lost to the mist more often than to his blade.

## The Furnace

The community's early favorite and the launch window's second documented killer. The Furnace's identity runs hotter (in every sense): an ability profile built around area pressure and zone control, with phases that escalate his capacity to deny space — the killer equivalent of the mist's map consumption, applied surgically. Where The Maniac wins chases, The Furnace wins rooms: his kit rewards killers who think about where survivors must go rather than where they are, and his phase escalation turns the objective zones and escape chokes into his personal kill-boxes. For survivors, The Furnace matchup inverts The Maniac's lesson: his rounds are lost to positioning — being caught in the zones he controls as his phases mature — and won by routing discipline.
